The problem

A company ships an LLM system, it passes testing, and then it drifts. A model version changes. Someone edits a prompt. New documents enter the index. An edge case that never mattered starts appearing at scale.

Today the repair loop is manual: a user complains, an engineer reads traces, guesses at a change, ships it, and hopes. Most teams have no regression suite for their AI system at all — so every fix is a coin flip, and the breakage it causes elsewhere stays invisible until a customer finds it.

What Delta does

Delta evaluates production behaviour continuously and can test a system without requiring any change to it — we point at the customer’s existing endpoint and extract what we need from the response. For a regulated buyer, whose vendor often isn’t permitted to touch the deployed system at all, that is the difference between being able to prove something and not.

Each evaluation runs the customer’s test suite across model, prompt and configuration permutations, scored by an independent evaluation model. The judge is deliberately not the model under test.

Automated today: instrumentation, evaluation, scoring, permutation sweeps, measurement. Human-assisted: proposing the fix, and turning a production failure into a permanent regression test. Nothing ships without a person signing off.

Every incident becomes a permanent test. After a year, the system holds the institutional memory of everything that ever broke in that company’s AI stack — and that corpus compounds with tenure.

Why this is defensible

Evaluation and observability platforms — Braintrust, LangSmith, Arize, Galileo — are well built and centred on measurement. They leave the two hardest steps to the engineer: deciding what to change, and knowing the change didn’t break something else.

Fix generation will commoditise; proving a change safe will not, because it requires the customer’s accumulated history. And the buyer is different: everyone above sells developer tools to AI engineers at AI-first companies. We sell to organisations that must survive a regulated buyer’s review, and cannot put customer prompts on someone else’s cloud.

Go to market

  • The wedge is a diagnostic. We instrument a customer’s agent and produce a failure analysis in 48 hours. €5–15k, free for the first cohort. It is the pitch and the proof at once.
  • The business is annual contracts priced per system under management — €30–100k, plus implementation where deployment sits inside the customer’s own infrastructure.
  • Not priced per event or per trace. That axis races to zero. A customer starts with the agent that broke and ends with all of them.
